Community focus group on health and daily life (fictional test transcript)
Date: 2026-09-14
Location: Example Valley Community Center, Springfield
Note taker: Wilhelmina Pruitt-Oyelaran

Facilitator: Thank you all for coming. I would like to hear what makes it hard, and what helps, to stay healthy where you live. Who wants to begin?

Odalys: I will start. For me the biggest thing is the rent. My landlord raised it again and I am working two jobs at the Brightwater Cannery just to keep the apartment on 41 Tamarack Lane. The apartment has mold in the bathroom and he will not fix it.

Thandiwe: Same here with the rent, honestly. Everybody I know is stretched. And the bus. The bus does not come after nine, so when my son got sick at night I could not get to the clinic.

Bao: Rent, yes. And I do not have a car, so the transportation is a problem for appointments. My cousin Bao Junior drives me sometimes. You can call me on 413-555-0142 if you want my number for the next meeting, or write to bao.tranvern@example.org.

Cormac: I want to say something different. I have been in recovery for three years. The opioid problem on my street is real, and the stigma is real too. People look at you differently when they know.

Facilitator: Thank you, Cormac. Others, what do you think about care itself?

Yasmin: I cannot afford my copay, so I skip the doctor. The cost of insurance and the bills pile up. When I call the clinic I wait on the phone forever and nobody speaks my language, so I need an interpreter and there is none.

Odalys: Yes, the language thing. My mother only speaks Portuguese and the paperwork is all in English. It is hard to find out what help exists.

Thandiwe: I will say what works, though. My neighbors look out for each other. Miss Ferreira-Quill next door watches my kids, and our church group brings food when someone is sick. That is the strength of this place.

Bao: The food pantry on Tamarack Lane helps, but the line is long and the bus is late, so I miss it.

Yasmin: I worry about stress too. I cannot sleep, I feel anxious all the time about money and the rent.

Cormac: The park near 41 Tamarack Lane is where I walk, but there are no sidewalks on the way and it does not feel safe at night.

Odalys: Safety, yes. And Thandiwe is right about the neighbors. Housing is the thing that would change my family's health the most.

Facilitator: Thank you. Anything else before we close?

Yasmin: Just that people trust the clinic more when someone from the community is there. Call me at 413-555-0177 if you need volunteers. My email is yasmin.orlandini@example.org.
